12 pitfalls and their guardrails

Discipleship group starter kit

OverviewPitfallsTrainingToolkitSources

These come up again and again across church research, ministry leaders and practitioners. Number 10 matters most for a workplace like yours.

1. It turns into a Bible study

What it looks like. Great discussion, lots of learning, little change. People leave knowing more but living the same.

Guardrail. Every meeting ends with each person naming one specific step of obedience (James 1:22). Next week starts by asking how it went.

Sources: Christian Today, 4 pitfalls (2016) · UMC Discipleship, the trouble with small groups (2016) · Blake Chrisman, why D-groups stop making disciples (2020)

2. It turns into a support group

What it looks like. The whole hour goes to problems and venting. Care is real, but Scripture and growth get crowded out.

Guardrail. Care for each other, then bring each need to the Word and to prayer. Refer deeper needs (grief, marriage, addiction, mental health) to a pastor or counselor.

Sources: Lifeway Research, 5 elements (2015) · Christian Today, 4 pitfalls (2016)

3. Catch-up eats the meeting

What it looks like. Highs and lows run 30 minutes, and prayer gets the last two.

Guardrail. Use the timed meeting rhythm in the Toolkit and rotate a timekeeper.

Sources: Discipleship.org, 7 pitfalls · Blake Chrisman, why D-groups stop making disciples (2020)

4. Accountability is either soft or forced

What it looks like. Soft: everyone says "fine" and nobody asks again. Forced: a new or shy member is pushed to confess before trust exists.

Guardrail. Sign a covenant so accountability is invited, not imposed. The facilitator goes first in honesty. Let depth grow with trust.

Sources: Discipleship.org, 7 pitfalls · Christian Today, 4 pitfalls (2016) · Greg Ogden, C.S. Lewis Institute (2008)

5. One hero leader carries everything

What it looks like. The group depends on one strong person. When their life changes, the group falls apart, and nobody else feels qualified to lead.

Guardrail. Everyone points to Christ, not the leader. Recruit a co-facilitator from inside the group and rotate facilitation from month two, so leadership is shared across the group.

Sources: Baptist Press, 7 silent killers (Feb 2026) · Greg Ogden, C.S. Lewis Institute (2008)

6. No plan to multiply

What it looks like. The group is healthy and closed. Two years on, it is the same people and no new groups.

Guardrail. Name multiplication in the first meeting. Set an end date in the covenant. A group of 8 to 10 multiplies by splitting into two groups of 4 or 5 and inviting new members. Before the end date, each member names who they will invite.

Sources: Baptist Press, 7 silent killers (Feb 2026) · Lifeway Research, 5 elements (2015) · GoMicro, dangers to disciplemaking (2024)

7. No clear win

What it looks like. People cannot say what the group is for, so each person defines growth on their own terms.

Guardrail. Teach the five marks of a disciple (Session 2) and review them together every quarter.

Sources: Lifeway Research, why plans stall (Apr 2026) · Baptist Press, 7 silent killers (Feb 2026)

8. Commitment slides

What it looks like. Missed weeks, skipped reading, "I didn't get to it." It spreads to the whole group.

Guardrail. Put attendance and prep in the covenant. When it slips, revisit the covenant together kindly and early.

Sources: Discipleship.org, 7 pitfalls · Baptist Press, 7 silent killers (Feb 2026)

9. Drifting from Scripture

What it looks like. Shortcuts creep in, like dropping memory verses, or one member steers the group toward a favorite book or idea.

Guardrail. Keep a shared Bible reading plan as the core text. Facilitators check in monthly with the ministry lead.

Sources: GoMicro, dangers to disciplemaking (2024) · Discipleship.org, 7 pitfalls

10. Your boss is in your group

What it looks like. A staff member shares a struggle in group, and it later colors how their supervisor sees their work. People stop being honest.

Guardrail. Never place a supervisor and a direct report in the same group. Participation is voluntary and never comes up in performance reviews.

Source: The awkward trinity of ministry roles (Feb 2026)

11. Care turns into control

What it looks like. Guilt, pressure, rigid rules, a leader seen as more spiritual than others, and people who feel they cannot leave.

Guardrail. Anyone can step out without penalty. Facilitators are accountable to the ministry lead. Encourage people to test everything against Scripture themselves.

Source: Probe Ministries, spiritual abuse

12. Mixed-gender groups stay shallow

What it looks like. Members hold back on the things that most need talking about.

Guardrail. Keep groups same-gender at 8 to 10 people, and use same-gender pairs or threes for the accountability portion.

Source: GoMicro, dangers to disciplemaking (2024)
